How often should a business post?

It’s the question we hear most. The honest answer: as often as you can keep up with for months, not weeks. Here’s how to pick the right number — and stick to it.

Consistency beats volume

Posting every day for two weeks and then disappearing for a month does more harm than posting three times a week, every week. Platforms reward accounts that show up reliably, and so do people — they follow you because they expect more.

A good starting point

  • Instagram Reels: 3–5 a week.
  • TikTok: 4–7 a week. It moves faster and rewards volume more than most.
  • YouTube Shorts: repost your best Reels and TikToks — 3–5 a week.
  • Facebook: share your Reels there too; it costs nothing extra.
  • Stories: most days, even if it’s just one quick update. Stories keep you in front of the people who already follow you.

If that feels like a lot, start with three videos a week on one platform. Get that habit locked in before adding more.

Make one video work five times

You don’t need five ideas for five platforms. Film one vertical video and post it to Reels, TikTok, Shorts and Facebook. Pull a quote from it for a Story. One session can fill a week.

Batch it, or it won’t happen

The businesses that stay consistent don’t film every day. They block out 60–90 minutes once a week, film four or five videos in a row, and schedule them. Ideas are easier in bulk, and you only have to set up once.

When to post

Your own analytics are better than any generic “best time to post” chart. Check when your followers are most active (Instagram shows this under Insights) and post an hour or so before the peak. Then test: try different times for two weeks and keep what works.

What matters more than frequency

Posting more bad videos won’t help. If people swipe away in the first two seconds, frequency just spreads the problem. Fix your hooks first, then increase how often you post.
