How to write a hook that stops the scroll

You have about two seconds before someone decides to keep watching or move on. The hook is how you win those two seconds — and it matters more than your camera, your editing, or your hashtags.

What a hook actually is

A hook is the first thing someone sees and hears in your video. It isn’t a greeting, a logo, or “so today I want to talk about…”. It’s a reason to stay. Good hooks do one of three things: they make people curious, they promise something useful, or they say something people feel strongly about.

The three layers of a hook

The best hooks work on three levels at once, so they land whether the sound is on or off:

  • What you say — the first sentence. Short, specific, and about the viewer.
  • What they read — the same idea as bold text on screen in the first frame. Most people start with the sound off.
  • What they see — movement or something unexpected. Start mid-action, not standing still waiting to talk.

Five hook formulas that work

  1. The mistake: “The ___ mistake almost everyone makes.” People hate the idea that they’re doing something wrong.
  2. The shortcut: “How to ___ in under 60 seconds.” A clear promise with a clear time cost.
  3. The result: “I tried ___ for 30 days. Here’s what happened.” Curiosity plus a story.
  4. The callout: “If you’re a ___, you need to hear this.” Speak to one person and the right people stop.
  5. The opinion: “___ is overrated.” A strong take gets comments — and comments get reach.

Common hook mistakes

  • Saving the good part for later. Lead with the most interesting moment, then explain.
  • Being vague. “Some tips for your business” loses to “3 ways to get more Google reviews this week”.
  • Introducing yourself first. Earn their attention, then tell them who you are.
  • Overpromising. If the video doesn’t deliver what the hook promised, people learn to skip you.

How to get better, fast

Write five hooks for every video and pick the best one. Film two or three takes of just the hook — it’s the part worth the extra effort. Then check your analytics: the percentage of people still watching after three seconds tells you exactly how good your hook was. Aim to beat your own number every week.
